Shove Knight

McGame Jame 2021

My buddy and I wanted to make a puzzle game, where the player creates a trail behind them that acts both as an obstacle (like in snake games) and as the primary method for clearing a level.

Our initial brainstorming led to a grid based enemy shoving puzzler. The floor falls behind the charcter as they navigate a grid and they need to shove the enemies into the newly create pits, before returning back to where they started.

We didn't know this at the time but we had unwittingly made a Sokoban-type game. Looking back now, I'm almost certain we had both played some similar games before coming together for this project. A lot of our design decisions converged to staples of this subgenre (such as having an easy way to undo a move or restart a level).

We intentionally kept the scope for this game as small as possible. My friend already had some boilerplate for grid based games on hand and we wanted to stick to a monochrome color palette, and as a result making the pixel art wasn't as challenging. After the first 24 hours we had pretty much all we needed to get started on churning out levels.

My process was to work backwards from a predrawn path on a grid, and to add enemies and keys along the way. Then, I would revisit the path and tweak some turns and obstacles here and there and iterate until I was happy with the result.

(note to self, come back and show process behind some of the levels)

There's some clever levels in there, but I would say a majority of them don't really give players a sense of breakthrough. As I played more block-pushers after this jam, I started to develop my instinct for puzzle design (hempuli's suite of mini games is great for this!). When it comes to polish, I'd also probably seriously tighten the interpolation time of everything in the game in a future update; at the moment everything just feels mushy.

If I haven't somehow convinced you to try a whole library of better sokoban-type games already, you can check out Shove Knight below!
