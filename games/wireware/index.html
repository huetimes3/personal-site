WireWare

GMTK Jam 2020

The first game I got to help design, and one of my favorites in terms of how the puzzles turned out. I had a friend work on the programming while I did the art, writing and bulk of the levels. I was so unfamiliar with unity at the time that I drew most of the levels on paper before trying to port them into the game.

The theme was "out of control", and we pretty quickly decided on making a puzzle game that relies on the common keyboard shortcuts of ctrl+v, ctrl+x and ctrl+c. The idea was to have levels that required moving keys around to press buttons that open up gates. By opening up the gates, you create a path to the goal.

Given the game's cyber theme, the gates themselves are "kinda sorta logic" gates. In practice, they can be hooked up to multiple buttons, with either an "any" or "all" rule (i.e. "any" of the buttons need to be pressed, or "all" the buttons need to be pressed). On top of that, there's also an optional "not" flag that can reverse the state of the gate. We give the players a few levels to experiment with this baseline ruleset.

After a few "normal" levels, the player is given ctrl+v with a keycard in their clipboard, and the challenge comes from thinking about where to place the card to make the level completable. Then, they're introduced to ctrl+x, and finally to ctrl+c. The decision behind this order of unlocks was clear, we wanted the players' to feel like each new move built upon the previous. Each unlock was also to some extent a stronger version of the previous (although some levels exploit the removing aspect of ctrl+x), and so the progression felt relatively natural.

I had loads of fun writing the malware's dialogue, and for a first attempt at serious pixelart I was pretty happy with the end result. The level progression was a bit all over the place. We had some friends playtest them and shifted the levels around based on their difficulty with each one. Still, after each unlock there's a few rather easy levels to get used to the introduced mechanics which is what led to the large apparent level count for a 48hr jam.

After playing it again a few years later, I'm pleasantly surprised by what we were able to pull off. It's scrappy and missing a lot of the polish we wish we could have had the time to implement, but some of the levels actually had me stumped!
